Building an Adaptable Shopping Experience

📝 About

As more and more customers turn to online retailer for their shopping needs, especially during the past few years, Tapestry needed to fully reimagine the shopping experience of our sites. By delivering a digital experience that is more personalized and curated to each of our customers, we believe that it would be the key differentiator for our brands from their competitors.

Problem Statement

The lack of personalized experiences for customers visiting our sites is impeding their ability to quickly and confidently find products, leading to extended search times. This deficiency in personalized guidance may potentially result in decreased conversions for our sites.

How might we tailor the shopping experience specific to each customer based on their unique needs and preferences?

💭 Who are we designing for?

Focus area

Utilizing insights from previous research studies, the UXR team has identified four primary Customer Purchase Missions. Our focus for the upcoming two quarters was on the “I’m looking for a specific product functionality” purchase mission.

  • I’m looking for a specific product functionality
  • I’m just browsing
  • I’m shopping for an occasion
  • I’m hunting for a deal

I then collaborated with the UXR team to create a high-level customer journey for this mission, and we decided to focus on which are the Browsing and Evaluation Phases. These critical stages play a decisive role in determining whether or not a customer chooses to make a purchase from us.

  1. Pre-site visit

    Coming from Search, Social, or Direct

  2. Browsing

    Navigate between the Product Listing and Product Detail pages to find what they like

    Customer quotes:

    • “Scrolling down the web page makes the list seem endless.”
    • “I would like to look at the details of multiple products before I add to bag.”

    Customer needs:

    • Confident that they can easily narrow down their options
    • Confident that they can evaluate what they see

  3. Evaluation

    Learn more about the product to make a decision

    Customer quotes:

    • “I like the product videos that show what can fit in the bag.”
    • “The price has gone up and it’s more than I want to pay.”

    Customer needs:

    • Confident that the product meet their requirements
    • Understand how it would it fit within their closet / style

  4. Checkout

    Adds to cart, but wants to wait for a sale to purchase

Data

About 60% of customers made a transaction after 2 or more sessions. However, the largest bucket is customers who made a transaction on their first session

Based on this insight, it is imperative to address the needs of both in-session users and returning customers, making sure that all customers get a personalized experience as soon as they land on our sites.

📚 Learning Sprints

During the same time, the Product and UX team also introduced a new way of working called “Learning Sprints”. The goal is to ideate and test different concepts with our customers and get learnings from them every week. These insights will then guide our decisions on which concepts to incorporate into A/B tests for potential launch.

✨ Design solutions

Design solution

Shopping Guide

We often relate to how in-store sale associates are able to guide customers to find the right product for themselves and think about how we can bring a similar experience to our sites.

One way to accomplish that is to have a shopping guide widget that asks customers what they’re looking for and shows relevant products as they answer.

Feedback from users:

  • 5 of the 6 Participants rated it 5 out of 5 in terms of helpfulness.
  • Most participants found the the guide natural and easy to use.
  • They also appreciated the fact that it stayed on the screen as they were filtering down and that the questions were helpful and nuanced.

Impact:

Page conversion rate on a product listing page increased by 2.8%, which leads to an estimated revenue of ~$700,000

Design solution

Similar Options

After conducting both qualitative and quantitative research, we identified two primary issues with our current recommendation container on the Product Listing Page:

  1. Customers generally find the recommendation container on our Product Page unhelpful and lacking personalization.
  2. 30% of customers only engage with the product image carousel before leaving the page.

To build more trust with our customers around our recommendations, I explored creating recommendations based on specific attributes of the product that customer has viewed. By doing so, we can provide clarity on why exactly we’re recommending each product to our customers.

Additionally, I examined the positioning of the "View Similar" button with the aim of capturing customers at the opportune moment, leveraging insights from their browsing behavior. The two placements I explored are:

  • The last slot of the product image carousel
  • On the product tile of the viewed product, once they go back to the Product Listing Page

Impact:

The conversion rate saw a 3.42% increase, resulting in an annualized revenue of $4.5 million.

Design solution

Enhanced Recommendation Modules

A common piece of feedback we've received regarding recommendation modules is that customers don’t fully trust the modules to provide personalized recommendations. Besides improving our recommendation algorithm, I also wanted to build trust through the design of the modules.

One way to achieve this was by specifying why we’re recommending particular products. Using copy like "Because you viewed..." and a thumbnail of a previously viewed product gives customers more context on why certain products are being recommended to them, which in turn builds their trust.

Another module I worked on further expands on how adaptive we can make our recommendations. Based on all the products customers have interacted with, we can pull specific shared attributes across those products and use them as quick filters in the recommendation module.

Impact:

The conversion rate saw a 2.08% increase, resulting in an annualized revenue of $2.1 million.

Design solution

Filtered Prompts

One of the main themes we observed from the moderated user interviews is that our customers are hesitant of using the filter and prefer to just scroll through the listing page to learn more about the selection.

Our data also shows that conversion rate is lower without the use of filters.

Based on these findings, I looked into ways to meet our users where they are at and expose different filter options as users scroll down the listing page.

Filtered Prompts

Display most-used filters as prompts as customers are scrolling down the listing page.

Feedback from users:

  • Participants generally appreciated seeing visual cards as those help them better visualize the options.
  • The prompts are more helpful when they’re just in the browsing mood and not looking for anything specific

💭 Takeaways

  • The velocity of the Learning Sprint, which required rapid solutioning and iteration, forced me to really hone into each customer problem we’re trying to solve.
  • Due to the expectation of high velocity, a tight collaboration between UX, Research and Product was essential for this project to be successful.
